# Email marketing

## List building that stays legal and warm
- One list, one promise: the reason to join is stated at the signup, and the
  first email delivers it the same day.
- No bought lists, no scraped addresses, no "we will not spam" as the pitch.
  The pitch is the thing they get.
- Double opt in when the niche is spammy; clean list beats long list.

## Deliverability basics
- Authenticate the domain (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) before the first campaign; it is
  an afternoon of work that protects every send.
- New domain or long silence: warm up slowly, a few hundred a day, not the
  whole list at once.
- One click to unsubscribe, visible. Harder unsubscribes create spam marks,
  and spam marks are what actually kill the account.
- Watch the boring numbers: opens collapsing after a send means stop and fix,
  not send again louder.

## Flows that matter for small stores, in order
1. Welcome flow: three emails. Deliver the promise, show the best seller or
   best work, one human note from the owner.
2. Abandoned checkout: two emails. First one plain, "you left this", second
   answers the top objection or adds a small nudge. No instant discount;
   discounts train waiting.
3. Post purchase: thank you plus care tips, then the review ask at the moment
   the product has landed.
- That is it until those earn their keep. More flows before that is decoration.

## Subject lines and copy that do not look automated
- Say it like a person: short, specific, one idea. "Your order, and one thing
   I noticed" beats "EXCLUSIVE OFFER INSIDE".
- No false urgency, no name merge abuse, no emoji stack. One voice, the
   owner's, first person where true.
- One call to action per email. The second link halves the first.

## Campaign rhythm
- One campaign a week is plenty for a small store; consistency beats bursts.
- Segment by behaviour after the first month: buyers and browsers get
   different messages, because they are different people.
