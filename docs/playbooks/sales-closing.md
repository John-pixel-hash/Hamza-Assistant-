# Sales psychology and closing

## The frame
- You are selecting clients, not begging for them. Every message is written by
  someone who is booked, not someone who is free.
- Specific beats persuasive. "Delivery in 9 working days, first draft Thursday"
  closes; "fast delivery" does not.

## Objections, by type
- "Too expensive": never defend the number. Ask what it is compared against,
  then restate what is included and what it earns back. Offer the smaller
  package, not the smaller price.
- "I need to think": agree warmly, then fix the next step: "what is the one
  thing you need to be sure of?" Name it, answer it, propose the date.
- "I have a cheaper option": let them have it. One line on what changes at
  that price, then leave the door open without waiting by it.
- "Send me your portfolio first": send two relevant pieces, not everything,
  each with one line on the result it produced.

## Price mechanics
- Quote two numbers, never one: the full package and the sensible package.
  The brain compares them instead of comparing you against silence.
- Deposit before start, half is standard. A client who will not deposit is not
  a client, and saying so politely is a filter, not a loss.
- Commitment devices: a start date, a content due date, a launch date. Dates
  turn a maybe into a plan.

## Follow up cadence
- Day 0: reply with question and read back.
- Day 2: one line adding a concrete detail or idea for their project.
- Day 5: one line with a date you will start if they confirm this week.
- Then stop. A fourth message is spam and burns the review.

## When to walk away
- No deposit, repeated discounting, scope creep before start, or disrespect in
  the first three messages. Walk with a polite one liner; the slot goes to a
  better client.

## Decision tree: silence after a quote
1. Wait two days, send the idea message (value, not "just checking in").
2. Wait three more, send the date message.
3. Stop. Reopen only when they return or their season approaches.
