# Ecommerce and dropshipping

## Product selection reality checks
- Solves a visible problem or marks an identity; either one, not neither.
- Price band that survives ads: roughly 30 to 80 dollar retail, with room for
  a 3 times markup over landed cost. Cheaper than that and ads eat the margin.
- Not already everywhere at a lower price; search the product image before
  believing it is a winner.

## Supplier and shipping truth
- Shipping time stated on the product page, honestly. "7 to 12 days" written
  plainly beats "fast shipping" and a chargeback.
- Order one unit to the client's own address before selling. Photos lie;
  packaging and smell do not.
- One supplier per winner plus a named backup; stock outs during a winning ad
  are how accounts die.

## Store conversion essentials
- [ ] First screen says what it is, for whom, and the one reason to buy.
- [ ] Product page: five plus real photos or video, size and shipping facts,
      the objection handled in the description.
- [ ] Checkout tested on a phone, to the thank you page, by a human.
- [ ] Trust without clutter: payment marks, a real contact page, a returns
      line in plain words.

## Unit economics, before scaling
- Know the number: product cost plus shipping plus ad cost per order plus
      platform fees. What remains is the margin; if it is under a third of
      the price, fix economics before ad budget.
- AOV levers first: bundle, second unit at a cut, free shipping threshold.
  Raising the basket beats cutting the ad cost.

## Returns and support
- One page returns policy, in the client's voice, before the first order.
- Answer support within a day; a fast honest reply turns a return request
  into a review that is not a disaster.
- Track why returns happen and feed it back: sizing, photos, description
  promise. Returns are free market research.

## Decision tree: traffic but no sales
1. Add to cart rate below 2 percent: product page or price, not the ads.
2. Carts but no checkout: shipping shock or forced account creation.
3. Checkout started but not finished: payment options or a form too long.
4. All healthy and still nothing: the offer. Compare the price against the
   identical product one search away.
