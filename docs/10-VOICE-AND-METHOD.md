## 1. Evidence from your own archive first

Before accepting or rejecting any advice, I tested one claim against your real
Instagram history. The purchased "sales closer" prompt says to be relentless and
never take no for an answer. Your own documents say never chase. Only one of
them can be right, and you already have the data to settle it.

**Threads grouped by how many messages you sent before they replied at all:**

| Messages you sent | Threads | Replied | Reply rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 35 | 20 | **57%** |
| 2 | 35 | 9 | **26%** |
| 3 or more | 315 | 2 | **1%** |

**Threads where you sent a "did you see my message" follow up:**

| | Threads | Reply rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| With a chase message | 356 | **5%** |
| Your overall rate | 393 | **10%** |

Read that second table carefully. The chase message performs at **half** your
normal rate.

The cost, in your own effort: you sent **316 messages** that were a third
attempt or later, and they earned **2 replies**. That is roughly **158 messages
written per reply**.

**The honest caveat.** This is observation, not a controlled experiment. People
who never replied were colder from the start, which is exactly why you sent them
another message. So the chase message did not necessarily *cause* the silence.
But the practical conclusion survives that caveat either way: **a third message
to a person who did not answer two does not convert them.** The time is better
spent on a stronger first message, or on the 9 conversations sitting there
waiting for you.

This is why your own rule wins, and it is now the system's rule: **one follow
up, then leave it.**

## 2. Where your documents agree, and this is the core method

All three primary documents describe the same method. That agreement is worth
more than any single technique:

1. **Understand before offering.** Situation, problem, what they have tried.
2. **Surface the real problem**, including what happens if nothing changes.
3. **Give value before talking price.** A free audit, a review, an insight.
4. **Price only after value**, and always with a reason attached.
5. **One ask per message.** One question. One next step.
6. **Short by default.** Long only when detail is genuinely needed.
7. **Never chase.** Calm authority. They came to you.
8. **Set the scope in writing before starting.** This is the single highest value
   habit in the whole set, and it is what prevents almost every dispute later.
9. **Fifty percent up front, minimum.** No final files before final payment.
10. **After delivery: thank them, stay available, ask for a review.**

## 3. Your voice, as the documents describe it

These are your own rules, taken as binding:

- Never open with a validating phrase. No "That is great", "That is totally
  fine", "That is smart", "Great question", "Absolutely", "No worries at all".
- Never open with the client's name in a cold message.
- Short replies by default.
- **One follow up question per reply.** Not two.
- Never mention price before trust and value exist.
- Every message moves the conversation one step forward.
- Match their tone.
- Their problem is your pitch. Let them tell you what they need.
- Never push twice. If they say no, leave the door open and move on.
- No budget means exit clean, door left open.
- **Reply in their language.** English, French or Spanish.
- No bullet points, bold text or headers inside a chat message.
- Never "I think I can probably help". Say what you will deliver.

## 4. Five conflicts, with my recommendation

Your documents disagree with each other in five places. Each one needs a
decision, and I have recommended one. Nothing here is built until you agree.

### Conflict 1: How many follow ups

- Your guide: maximum 2 to 3.
- altin prompt: one follow up only, then leave it.

**Recommendation: one.** The evidence in section 1 supports it, and it protects
your Instagram account from being reported as spam. A second follow up is
allowed only when there is a concrete reason: they asked a question and went
quiet, or they agreed to a step and did not do it.

### Conflict 2: What counts as a follow up

- Your guide has three ready made follow ups, including "did you get a chance to
  look at my last message".
- Your own data says that exact message performs at half your normal rate.

**Recommendation: replace it.** A follow up should add something: a specific
observation, a useful answer, or a new piece of information. "Just checking in"
adds nothing and measurably underperforms. The system will not generate it.

### Conflict 3: Where payment goes

- Your guide says Fiverr protected payment, or Payoneer.
- altin prompt says always through Fiverr, never outside for new clients.

**Recommendation: stay on Fiverr for anyone who came from Fiverr.** Moving a
Fiverr client off the platform is against Fiverr's terms and is the kind of thing
that gets an account restricted or banned. That is your income at risk, not a
style preference. Payoneer is fine for clients you met elsewhere.

I am flagging this specifically because your guide offers Payoneer as an equal
option, and for a Fiverr sourced client it is not.

### Conflict 4: How hard to push

- Your guide, altin prompt, and Saint Creative guide all say never chase, hold
  your price, exit clean.
- The Alex prompts say be relentless, never accept no, and help them find money.

**Recommendation: your own documents win.** Three of your sources agree with each
other and with your data. The Alex prompts lose this one.

### Conflict 5: Urgency

- Alex prompts: create urgency, limited slots, price increasing soon.
- Everything else: calm authority, no pressure.

**Recommendation: real urgency only.** If your capacity is genuinely limited, or
the client set their own deadline, saying so is honest and it works. Invented
scarcity is the thing that makes a freelancer sound like a marketer, and clients
who have been burned before notice it immediately.

## 5. What I am not building, and why

I want to be direct rather than quietly soften these.

### Fabricated case studies and client stories

The Alex prompt instructs: "Use stories, analogies, and specific client examples
(fabricate realistic ones based on context)". **I am not building that.**

Inventing a client you never had, or results you never achieved, is
misrepresentation. If a prospect asks to speak to that client, or checks, you
lose the deal and your reputation in the same moment. It also puts your Fiverr
account at risk, since it is a platform where reviews and history are verifiable.

**What replaces it:** a real proof library. You already have real material. You
reviewed a live Shopify store and found genuine problems in it. You have a
portfolio. As real testimonials arrive they go in. The system uses your real
proof, and when there is no real proof for a claim, it does not make the claim.

### Encouraging clients into debt

The Alex prompt suggests helping buyers "find ways to get the funds (financing,
credit cards, borrowing from family/friends)" for a purchase that starts at $80.
**I am not building that.**

Pushing someone to borrow money for a small purchase is how you create a client
who regrets paying you. Their first message after delivery will be a refund
request. It also contradicts your own "exit clean, leave the door open" rule.

**What replaces it:** if budget is real, the honest move is a smaller starting
point, as the altin prompt already says. Never a discount, and never a loan.

### The "Alex Rivera, 25 years experience" persona

You are Hamza Nuhu. You have real work, real reviews and a real portfolio.
Telling a client you have twenty five years of experience when you do not is a
lie that falls apart the first time someone asks a follow up question.

**What replaces it:** confidence without invention. You do not need a fake
biography to speak with authority about work you have actually done.

### Relentless pursuit

Covered in section 1 with your own data. Chasing costs you time and risks your
account, and it does not work.

### What I am keeping from the Alex prompts

These parts are good, and they appear nowhere else:

- **Diagnose deeply before prescribing.** Ask one or two questions, not five.
- **Redirect early price questions.** "Depends what you need exactly. Tell me a
  bit more and I can give you a real number."
- **Tiered offers.** Give good, better, best, so the client chooses which level
  rather than whether to buy. This is legitimate and it is effective.
- **Summary close and assumptive close.** Restating what was agreed and then
  moving to the next step is normal, professional, effective selling.
- **Keep replies under four or five sentences** unless detail was requested.
- **Lead with what it means, then justify with the facts.**

## 6. The situation playbook

Tone is not enough. The situation decides the reply, and this is what makes the
difference between an assistant and a template engine. From your documents, the
situations worth recognising:

| Situation | The correct move |
| --- | --- |
| Cold first contact | One question about where they are. No pitch. |
| They ask price immediately | Redirect once, then answer after one qualifying question |
| They say it is too expensive | Ask their budget. Adjust scope, never the rate. Hold. |
| They found it cheaper | Do not match. Explain what is different, calmly. |
| Silence after a quote | One follow up that adds value, then leave it |
| Scope creep | It is outside what we agreed, so it is a small extra charge. Warm, not defensive. |
| Third revision request | Address in scope without complaint, price out of scope |
| Payment due | Clear, direct, no apology. Fifty percent up front. |
| Complaint or our mistake | Acknowledge first, fix second, no excuses |
| Off platform request | Behaviour depends on where the client came from. See Conflict 3. |
| Repeat buyer | Different relationship. Reference the last project. |
| Not a real buyer | Recognise it early and do not spend the time |

6. **A proof library** replaces fabrication, seeded with your real portfolio and

7. **Follow up discipline in the pipeline.** After one unanswered follow up the
   app stops suggesting more, and says so plainly.
